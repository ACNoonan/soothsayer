# §2 — Related Work

Our contribution sits at the intersection of three literatures: decentralised-oracle design, cross-venue price discovery, and the calibration / conformal-prediction framework for interval forecasts. Pairs of these have been joined before, and we position against the two closest joins explicitly: conformal prediction has been applied to the oracle problem, as Byzantine-robust consensus across concurrently-reporting sources [acon2-2023] (§2.1); and regime-dependent conformal recalibration has been applied to one-sided tail-risk forecasting [schmitt-rwc-2026; zhong-proxy-2026] (§2.3). What we are not aware of in prior work is a *calibration* claim on an oracle's served value during the hours its reference market is **closed** — the setting in which the uncertainty is structural rather than adversarial, and no quorum of honest reporters can reduce it. A fourth literature — institutional model-risk-management guidance — supplies the disclosure vocabulary we adopt.

## 2.1 Decentralised oracle designs

The deployed oracle landscape is organised around an *integrity* primitive: can the oracle deliver a faithful value on-chain without a trusted intermediary? Chainlink's decentralised-oracle-network architecture [chainlink-2] aggregates off-chain reports from a node-operator committee, with Data Streams [chainlink-streams] providing pull-based delivery and an explicit `marketStatus` flag — shipped in three co-existing schemas as of 2026-Q2: v8 RWA Standard [chainlink-v8], v10 Tokenized Asset [chainlink-v10], and v11 RWA Advanced [chainlink-v11]. v8 is the load-bearing path for tokenised-equity lending on Solana: Kamino Finance ($4B TVL) consumes every xStock collateral asset through Kamino Scope's `OracleType::ChainlinkRWA` adaptor [scope-oracle-type; kamino-xstocks-gov], routed through the v8 dispatcher, with two parallel feeds per xStock registered in Scope's mainnet config [scope-mainnet-cfg] — an `Open` mode that refuses updates outside US trading hours and an `AllUpdates` mode clamped to ±500 bps of the Open feed's Friday-close value. v10 backs the Backed↔Solana xBridge but is not consumed by the canonical Solana lending market. Pyth's Solana surface [pyth-agg; pyth-conf] takes a first-party-publisher approach: each permissioned publisher submits a price and confidence interval; the aggregate rule assigns three votes per publisher (price, price ± confidence) before taking the median. Pyth Pro [pyth-pro] layers subscriber-customisable cadence and the Blue Ocean ATS overnight integration; since September 2025 Pyth has been the exclusive on-chain distributor of Blue Ocean's executable book through end-2026 [blueocean-pyth], covering 8:00 PM – 4:00 AM ET Sun–Thu (the canonical xStock weekend remains uncovered). CF Benchmarks (Kraken-owned, FCA + BMR-supervised) launched the xStocks Indices product family on 2026-05-07 [cfb-xstocks-indices; cfb-methodology-guide] — regulated per-second scalars per xStock (Price Return + Total Return variants) powering Kraken's tokenised-equity perpetual futures; the methodology is FCA-supervised but emits no confidence, band, or coverage field on the wire. SEDA Protocol's SEDA Benchmark family [seda-benchmark] publishes session-aware single-asset feeds and a USA500 composite (60% equities / 25% crypto / 15% commodities) consumed by Hyperliquid HIP-3 markets via Dreamcash, Injective, and Perps.Fun derivatives venues, governed by a four-pillar continuity rule (session tags, cost-of-carry futures-to-spot, self-referencing EMA, staleness fallback) with no on-wire uncertainty field. Switchboard [switchboard] generalises aggregation into permissionless queues but has shallow tokenised-equity deployment; RedStone Live [redstone-live] blends institutional feeds with perpetual-market data; optimistic oracles UMA [uma] and Tellor [tellor] replace continuous aggregation with dispute-and-stake.

None of these designs publishes a *calibration claim* on the served value. The deployed-surface evidence is categorical at the wire: v8 RWA Standard is mid-only (no `bid`/`ask`/confidence) with `marketStatus` as the only signal of off-hours regime; v11's weekend `bid` carries a synthetic `.01`-suffix at 100% incidence on three of four mapped xStocks; v10 carries no `bid`/`ask`/confidence field and serves a 24/7 `tokenizedPrice` whose CEX-aggregation methodology is qualitatively described; CFB's BMR Article 27 Benchmark Statement contains no language about realised coverage; SEDA's four-pillar continuity methodology yields a single scalar plus session-flag metadata; Pyth's `(price, conf)` reports low publisher disagreement at venue close. Pyth's documentation [pyth-conf] asserts ~95% coverage at the *publisher* level, not as a property of the aggregate. Optimistic oracles guarantee *eventual* correctness under economic security but do not define a distributional statement conditional on time-of-week or realised volatility. Because none of these surfaces emits a calibrated coverage band, the contribution is necessarily categorical: a coverage-vs-sharpness numerical comparison is not well-defined against them and is therefore not reported.

The academic literature has similarly concentrated on integrity. Angeris and Chitra [angeris-cfmm] prove conditions under which CFMMs are incentive-compatible as price oracles. Eskandari et al.'s SoK [sok-oracles] catalogues oracle-manipulation attacks; the framing is adversarial (*did the attacker corrupt the value?*) rather than statistical (*over $N$ windows, did the served band contain the truth at the claimed rate?*). Luo et al.'s more recent SoK of RWA tokenisation [sok-rwa-2026] systematises the architecture and legal-interoperability layer of the tokenised-asset stack, but treats the reference price as an input to the system rather than as a quantity carrying its own calibration statement. The BIS bulletin [bis-oracle] frames the tension as decentralisation-versus-efficiency; on-chain consumers operate under adversarial ordering [flashboys-2], amplifying the cost of any systematic band miscalibration.

The closest prior work in *form* is ACon² [acon2-2023], which applies online conformal prediction to the oracle problem directly: it derives a **consensus set** from multiple oracle contracts via online uncertainty quantification, with a correctness guarantee that holds under distribution shift and Byzantine adversaries, demonstrated on Ethereum DEX/CEX price feeds with a Solidity implementation. The output is interval-valued and the machinery is conformal — yet the primitive remains integrity. The width of an ACon² set answers *how far apart are concurrently-reporting sources, and might some of them be lying?*: every source is live and observable, and the uncertainty is **reducible** by adding honest reporters. Our setting inverts each of those conditions. There is one reference market and it is closed; no quorum of honest reporters can observe Monday's open on a Sunday afternoon; the uncertainty is structural and irreducible rather than adversarial; and the served quantity is a consumer-requested coverage level with a re-derivable receipt, not a set asserted to contain the honest majority. ACon² is therefore the sharpest available demonstration that the integrity/calibration distinction is not a strawman — a conformal oracle that nonetheless makes no calibration claim about a closed reference market.

Our contribution is orthogonal. The integrity primitive asks whether $\hat P_t \approx P_t^{\text{true}}$; the calibration primitive asks, given the oracle's own band, whether it contains $P_t^{\text{true}}$ at the claimed rate. An oracle can be integrity-perfect and calibration-meaningless: Chainlink faithfully reports Friday's 16:00 close on Sunday at 14:00, but that value is not asserted to be a 95%-coverage estimate of Monday's open. We invert the interface so $\tau$ is a request parameter and $q_\text{served}$ is the receipt.

## 2.2 Cross-venue price discovery

The base forecaster underlying our calibration surface is not methodologically novel; its point estimate is a factor-switchboard scaling of Friday's close by a weekend futures return. The choice of futures as the leading venue applies Hasbrouck's information-share methodology [hasbrouck-1995; hasbrouck-2003], which establishes that most price discovery in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 occurs in E-mini futures — the statement we rely on when scaling SPY and QQQ Friday closes by ES and NQ weekend returns. Earlier work [stoll-whaley-1990; mackinlay-ramaswamy-1988] established intraday lead-lag and mispricing-adjustment dynamics in index-futures markets; Gonzalo–Granger [gonzalo-granger] is the conventional complement.

The Friday 16:00 ET to Monday 09:30 ET weekend is the longest contiguous non-trading interval in the US calendar. French [french-1980] documents the Monday-effect, establishing the weekend as a distinct return-generating regime. Barclay and Hendershott [barclay-hendershott-2003] show after-hours price discovery is non-trivial, so a stale-hold feed is systematically miscalibrated; their companion paper [barclay-hendershott-2004] shows after-hours effective spreads are 3–4× regular-hours, reflecting greater adverse selection — exactly the window in which a protocol holding an RWA as collateral is most exposed. For ETFs, Madhavan and Sobczyk [madhavan-sobczyk-2016] characterise creation/redemption-driven discovery, validating our ETF-class instruments within a unified frame.

A newer thread addresses the structural migration motivating this work. Makarov and Schoar [makarov-schoar-2020] document cross-venue arbitrage in cryptocurrency markets; Lehar and Parlour [lehar-parlour-2025] show Ethereum DEXs do not exhibit long-lived arbitrage relative to centralised benchmarks. Cong et al. [cong-tokenized-2025] provide the first large-scale empirical study of tokenised stocks (top-100 Backed/Ondo issuance, July–October 2025, $420M aggregate market cap, 14× growth in the four-month window). Four findings establish the empirical premise of the closed-market problem this paper addresses. **(a)** During overlapping US-market hours, tokenised prices track underliers near-one-for-one — a 1% move in the underlying translates to a 0.982% move in the token with adjusted $R^2 = 0.982$ [Table 6, p.34]; the tokenised-vs-underlying gap is a closed-market phenomenon, not a general informational degradation. **(b)** During the closed-market window the wedge is empirically substantial: TSLAx — the panel's most-traded name and a live Kamino collateral asset — deviates from the last underlying close by more than 1% in 71% of weekday off-hours and 15% of weekend hours; by more than 2% in 57% / 8%; by more than 5% in 12% / 0% [Table 4, p.32]. **(c)** Off-hour token returns predict the close-to-open underlying return at $\lambda = 0.903$ with adjusted $R^2 = 0.839$ [Table 10, p.40], but the relationship is regime-dependent — informative on weekends (Table 9, p.39: highest-quintile-minus-lowest-quintile spread of $+0.77\%$ on the subsequent Monday open) and reversal-driven on weekday overnights (Table 8, p.38: spread of $-0.99\%$, $t \approx -2.7$). The signal lives in the conditional mean, not the conditional quantile, and consumers cannot tell from a single tokenised-side scalar which regime they are in. **(d)** The wedge persists by structural design — issuer-controlled redemption windows, KYC/AML constraints on minting/redemption, and primary-arbitrageur geofencing (Backed under European sandbox exemptions, Ondo geofenced from US individuals) [§3.3.3, p.17; §6.1, pp.41–42] — and is therefore not a transitional inefficiency. The signal Cong et al. document is conditional-mean; the risk-side complement — a conditional-quantile band that lets a consumer use the off-hour signal without inheriting the prints that "likely would have triggered halts in regulated markets" [cong-tokenized-2025, pp.6–7] — is the primitive this paper deploys. TSLAx's integration as Kamino Finance collateral (p.20) and its 14% premium relative to the prior underlying close in the September 13–15 2025 window (p.24) is the live anchor on which the consumer SLA is binding.

## 2.3 Calibrated and conformal prediction

Kupiec [kupiec-1995] proposed the unconditional-coverage LR test for VaR; Christoffersen [christoffersen-1998] generalised to conditional coverage, decomposing into Kupiec plus an independence test on the hit sequence. The $p_{UC}$ and $p_{\text{ind}}$ columns we report are these tests; we use the pair rather than the joint because they correspond in banking practice to the Basel traffic-light framework [bcbs-backtest-1996]. The density-forecast generalisation [diebold-gunther-tay-1998] evaluates whether PITs are i.i.d. uniform; Berkowitz [berkowitz-2001] refines this to a small-sample-robust joint LR. Our coverage check at fixed $\tau$ is a marginal slice of the PIT framework, adopted because the protocol-consumer SLA is per-$\tau$.

Gneiting, Balabdaoui, and Raftery [gneiting-calibration-2007] state the modern objective: maximise sharpness subject to calibration. This organises our §7 ablation. Allen et al. [allen-tail-2025] formalise *tail calibration*; the deployed architecture closes a $\tau = 0.99$ tail-calibration ceiling that earlier hybrid-forecaster predecessors exhibited (§6.3, §7.2). The tradition traces to Brier [brier-1950].

The deployed architecture is a locally-weighted Mondrian split-conformal predictor [vovk-mondrian-2003; vovk-book-2005]: per-regime quantiles $q_r(\tau)$ on per-symbol pre-Friday $\hat\sigma_s$-standardised residuals, served after a multiplicative OOS-fit $c(\tau)$. (The §7.2 unweighted-Mondrian comparator omits the per-symbol standardisation; the walk-forward $\delta(\tau)$ schedule that an unweighted comparator requires collapses to identity once σ̂ is per-symbol.) Mondrian conformal preserves finite-sample marginal validity within each category under within-category exchangeability. Gibbs, Cherian and Candès [gibbs-conditional-2025] give the modern treatment that subsumes marginal, Mondrian/group, and localised conditional coverage in one framework; the deployed taxonomy is the discrete-partition case, chosen because each category must be computable on-chain from published state (§8) rather than for statistical optimality. A next-generation upgrade replaces the four-anchor schedule with a continuous conformal correction over the residual CDF — Romano-Patterson-Candès CQR [romano-cqr-2019] is the closest match. For non-exchangeability, Barber et al. [barber-nexcp-2023] extend via weighted quantiles, and Xu-Xie [xu-xie-2021] develop EnbPI for time-series.

**Conformal calibration of one-sided tail risk.** A 2026 thread applies conformal recalibration directly to one-sided VaR and is the closest methodological neighbourhood to the architecture deployed here. Schmitt's regime-weighted conformal calibration (RWC) [schmitt-rwc-2026] wraps an arbitrary quantile forecaster and calibrates an additive safety buffer from past forecast errors, weighted by exponential time decay and a regime-similarity kernel over market features; notably, the coverage gap is bounded for *arbitrary data-driven weights* by conditioning on the regime path and exploiting total-variation smoothness of the score distribution, rather than by assuming weighted exchangeability with respect to the algorithm's own weights. It is evaluated on the CRSP value-weighted index and sixteen CRSP-derived portfolios (1990–2024) against adaptive conformal inference [gibbs-aci-2021], DtACI [gibbs-dtaci-2022] and conformal PID control [angelopoulos-pid-2023] — the online-conformal family that also includes Zaffran et al.'s aggregated variant [zaffran-agaci-2022]. Zhong [zhong-proxy-2026] parameterises a different axis, a proxy-reliance exponent $\rho \in [0,1]$ applied to the volatility proxy in the nonconformity score, $u_s^{(\rho)} = (Y_s - \hat q_{\alpha,s}) / v_s^{\rho}$, interpolating between an approximately constant additive shift ($\rho = 0$) and a fully proxy-scaled correction ($\rho = 1$), and reports that intermediate $\rho$ is more robust when the proxy underreacts in stress. Adjacent work conformalises a quantile-regression-forest VaR [wang-qrf-var-2026], wraps a time-series conformal layer evaluated with the same Kupiec/Christoffersen pair adopted here [tcp-2025], and constructs one-sided intervals carrying tail-specific as well as global marginal validity [cuonzo-tail-2026].

Three things separate that thread from this paper. **(i) Regime enters by partition, not by weight or exponent.** RWC weights the calibration sample continuously by regime similarity and Zhong's $\rho$ rescales a single pooled score; neither partitions the calibration set. The deployed architecture fits *separate* per-regime quantiles $q_r(\tau)$ and standardises within partition by a per-symbol $\hat\sigma_s$. Expressed in Zhong's parameterisation the deployed score sits at $\rho = 1$ and the §7.1 constant-buffer comparator at $\rho \approx 0$, so the endpoints of that axis are ablated here while its interior is not (§10). **(ii) The panel is cross-sectionally heterogeneous and the claim is per-symbol.** Both papers evaluate index- or portfolio-level series, where cross-sectional scale heterogeneity is aggregated away before calibration; the per-symbol Kupiec claim of §6.4 is precisely the object that §7.2 shows an unweighted comparator fails while pooling to nominal. **(iii) The consumer and the prediction target differ.** Both recalibrate a risk manager's internal VaR on a continuously-trading daily series; here $\tau$ is a third party's request parameter answered on-chain with a re-derivable receipt (§3, §8), and the target is a closed-market gap whose regime taxonomy is partly *calendar*-structured — long weekends, scheduled earnings nights (§4.3, §5.5) — rather than a volatility dichotomy alone.

## 2.4 Institutional model risk management

The validation evidence published here matches the form required of any bank integrating an on-chain oracle into a collateral-valuation pipeline under SR Letter 11-7 [sr11-7] / SR 26-2 [sr26-2] and the Basel 1996 backtesting framework [bcbs-backtest-1996] — whose mathematical content is the Kupiec test §6.3 reproduces.
